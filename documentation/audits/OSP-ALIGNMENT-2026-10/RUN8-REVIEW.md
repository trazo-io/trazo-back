# Trazo Run 8: product, usability and trust review

Review date: 5 October 2026. Live observations across the landing site, authenticated Calapooia Bend account and published Duke buyer page. This is a product review, not certification, legal advice, or another full packet-conformance audit.

## Verdict

The promise is compelling: records entered during the season become renewal and inspection documents. The active renewal packet really downloaded, was automatically archived in Documents, and clearly distinguished a frozen filing from current supporting records. That is a tangible reason to consider $249/year.

The live experience does not yet communicate that promise consistently. The manager encounters several competing definitions of “complete”; the crew lead encounters long, partly English forms and invented template quantities; the buyer encounters strong self-reporting disclosures followed by stronger, unsupported assurances. Those contradictions matter more than cosmetic redesign.

I would fix record-entry integrity and claim consistency before broad October outreach, then run short observed tasks with actual growers. Willingness to pay is an inference here, not validated demand: no target customer was interviewed or observed in this review.

**This is not a claim of complete A–H coverage.** Authenticated, public and recipient experiences were substantially explored, including a downloaded recipient audit dossier. True phone-width/offline testing and live voice capture remain untested. The exact boundaries are below.

## Claims and trust: specific cleanup list

These are the claim families actually encountered, not a declaration that every page on every domain has been checked.

- **Permission:** Methodology's unapproved-input sentence is the urgent correction (R8-01). An immediate-notification duty is not permission to apply an otherwise prohibited input.
- **Scores/verification:** Photo verification, percentage penalties, compliance PASS/REVIEW/FAIL, and narrative “100% substantively complete” contradict the stated product position (R8-02, R8-10). A section having text is not compliance verification.
- **Approval:** “Approved inputs” on a plan, organic-approved grower-reported badges, and lookup outcomes need visible bases. The grower-reported qualifier is a strength; keep it. Do not promote catalog inclusion, plan membership or a saved event into certifier approval (R8-08, R8-15, R8-26).
- **Independent assurance:** The certification/audit category labels a grower's notice as an independent check; its icon treatment compounds the mismatch (R8-15).
- **Completeness:** “Every step,” “full traceability,” “nobody can hide” and “every application carries” promise more than the demonstrated record set (R8-16, R8-22).
- **Outcomes:** Weighed/graded, crop-saving frost work, training guarantees and economic-necessity assertions are not justified by an event type alone (R8-09).
- **Carbon:** Good boundary disclosure is undermined by expanded placement, “Your Impact,” an incompletely expressed unit and an inconsistent saved cleanout calculation (R8-04, R8-14). No observed premium-price promise or buyer-facing blockchain claim is reported.
- **Regulatory equivalence:** “The content is the same” for an agent without a register and the universal copper limit need correction (R8-12, R8-23).
- **Proof/privacy:** A live demo is not a customer case study; structured privacy does not remove purchasing references from public free text (R8-18, R8-19).
- **Unsubstantiated in this review:** five-year retention, comprehensive missing-field detection and electronic-record regulatory conformity need separately documented evidence. They are not proven false merely because this review could not test them (R8-22).

### Regulatory framing

The FTC Green Guides emphasize the net impression of environmental claims and clear, prominent qualification of certifications/seals. A small disclaimer does not automatically resolve stronger nearby assurance language. That makes the buyer-page hierarchy and badges substantive trust issues, not just copy preferences. This is a risk assessment, not an adjudicated violation.

Directive (EU) 2024/825 adds restrictions on unsupported sustainability labels and certain environmental claims; Member States were directed to apply implementing measures from 27 September 2026. Its application to Trazo depends on the relevant EU market and national implementation. I am not asserting that every US-only Trazo screen falls within it, nor that any green UI color alone is unlawful.

## Ten changes before October outreach, in order

1. **Remove misleading organic-use guidance and stale assurance methodology.** R8-01, R8-02, R8-23. Treat notification, permitted use, identity and approval as separate facts.
2. **Make logging fact-safe.** Remove unconfirmed template measurements; invalidate stale lookups; provide an explicit unknown basis. R8-03, R8-08.
3. **Make saved records read back faithfully.** Fix date-only/timezone inconsistencies and reconcile carbon preview with saved values. R8-04, R8-05.
4. **Stop generating unsupported facts and outcomes from event types.** R8-09. This matters both inside the app and publicly.
5. **Unify renewal readiness wording.** Remove the narrative percentage and unsupported-agent equivalence; make gaps consistent. R8-10, R8-12.
6. **Put renewal and inspection tasks in one obvious place.** Lead with current year, filing status, unanswered items and the correct export/review action; collapse revision history. Make recipient gaps navigable and non-grading. R8-11, R8-13, R8-27, R8-28.
7. **Finish the Spanish core logging flow and shorten it.** Task vocabulary, consistent tuteo, relevant fields/help, explain disabled voice, less intrusive onboarding. R8-06, R8-07, R8-24, R8-25, R8-26.
8. **Rebuild the buyer hierarchy around factual lot story and provenance.** Collapse carbon; separate reported records from independent certification; remove completeness/assurance badges. R8-14, R8-15, R8-16, R8-17.
9. **Make public proof and privacy promises precise.** Label specimens everywhere, qualify universal claims and show what public notes expose. R8-18, R8-19, R8-22.
10. **Sharpen the outreach conversion path.** Name the wholesale orchard/berry audience, show $249/year near the main promise, and surface Extra Farm beside Compliance. R8-20, R8-21. Then test the path with real target operations instead of inferring conversion from this expert review.

## What works and should remain

- The renewal-season hero and concrete promise are much stronger than generic sustainability positioning. Preserve them.
- Free forever/no card and the main annual prices are understandable. Preserve the simple pricing model.
- Exact prohibited-material lookup gave a prominent, understandable warning. Keep the ability to record what actually happened; improve the distinction between recording and permission rather than blocking truthful incident records.
- The buyer page actually opens without a consumer login. Its early self-reporting statement and explicit fictional-demo warning are excellent safeguards.
- Approximate rather than exact parcel location is clearly disclosed publicly. Keep it.
- The buyer's organic-input badge explicitly describes grower reporting rather than silently asserting a registry match. Preserve that provenance while changing stronger surrounding labels.
- Revision history and packet choice distinguish the current filed plan from a draft, including the different annual-update years. The menu explicitly warns a draft is a working copy.
- The downloaded active packet states missing sections, unsupported-agent scope and the limits of reconstructed revision 1. Its renewal supplement identifies revision replacement dates and says acknowledgments are not recorded.
- The packet downloaded successfully and appeared automatically in Documents with date and size. That is valuable visible automation.
- The finish-season form carried the test harvest's 20 lb forward. I did not finalize it or publish the ASTRA season.
- The existing sale display distinguishes typed numbers from supporting attachments. Missing evidence should remain visible rather than silently inferred.
- The authorized recipient link opened and downloaded a whole-operation dossier. Revocation was confirmed both by the owner-side success message and the recipient's “link no longer available” page. No link or message was sent.
